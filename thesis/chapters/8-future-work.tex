\chapter{Future Work}\label{chap:future_work}

This thesis has laid the groundwork for further exploration into the optimization of specialization mechanisms in Rust. Several avenues for future research and development present themselves:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Predicate Support}: As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, extending \mytool to support more predicates could enhance its expressiveness and utility.

	\item \textbf{Compiler Integration:} Further work could focus on integrating the specialization techniques directly into the Rust compiler, allowing for full stable support and wider adoption within the Rust ecosystem.
	      Once integrated, it would be interesting to explore optimizations that could be applied to the standard library and other widely used crates.

	\item \textbf{Performance Benchmarking:} While this thesis has provided initial benchmarks, a more extensive suite of tests across diverse real-world applications could yield deeper insights into the performance characteristics of the proposed optimizations.

	\item \textbf{Overlapping Implementations:} Investigating strategies to handle overlapping implementations more effectively could further enhance the robustness and applicability of the specialization mechanism, like the \textit{lattice} approach mentioned in section \ref{sec:method_wrapping}.

	\item \textbf{Broader Language Support:} The techniques developed in this thesis could be adapted for use in other programming languages that support macros, bringing the benefits of specialization to a wider audience.

	\item \textbf{User Studies:} Conducting user studies to assess the usability and developer experience when employing \mytool in real-world coding scenarios could provide valuable feedback for refinement.
\end{itemize}

By pursuing these directions, future work can build upon the foundation established in this thesis, advancing the state of specialization in Rust and potentially influencing other programming languages as well.
