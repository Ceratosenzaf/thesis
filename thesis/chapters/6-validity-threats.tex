\chapter{Threats to Validity}\label{chap:validity_threats}

We organize our discussion following Wohlin \textit{et al.}~\cite{Wohlin12}'s taxonomy.

% --------------------- Section 1 ---------------------

\section{Construct Validity}\label{sec:construct-validity}

\paragraph{Measurement of Key Variables}
Our study relies on specific metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of our approach.
If these metrics do not accurately capture the constructs we intend to measure, it could threaten the validity of our conclusions.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We carefully selected metrics that are widely accepted in the research community and relevant to our study objectives.

\paragraph{Similarity Criteria}
The criteria used to determine similarity between code snippets may not fully capture the nuances of code functionality and intent.
Hence, our similarity assessments might not reflect true equivalence in behavior.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We based our similarity criteria on established practices in code analysis and validated them through preliminary experiments.

% --------------------- Section 2 ---------------------

\section{Internal Validity}\label{sec:internal-validity}

\paragraph{Assumptions about AST Structure}
Our approach relies on certain assumptions about the structure of Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) generated by the Rust compiler.
If these assumptions do not hold for all codebases, it could affect the validity of our results.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We validated our assumptions by analyzing a diverse set of Rust projects and ensuring that our method works across different coding styles and patterns.
We also documented any limitations of our approach related to AST structure.

\paragraph{Instrumentation and Implementation Errors}
Errors in the implementation of our instrumentation or analysis tools could lead to incorrect results.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We conducted thorough testing of our tools, including unit tests and integration tests, to identify and fix potential bugs.
We also performed code reviews and sought feedback from peers to enhance the reliability of our implementation.

% --------------------- Section 3 ---------------------

\section{External Validity}\label{sec:external-validity}
\paragraph{Focus on open-source Rust projects}
Our evaluation is based on 65 open-source projects from GitHub and \texttt{crates.io}.
While these projects cover a broad spectrum of real-world software, they may not capture the full variability of proprietary or industrial codebases, which could limit the generalizability of our findings.

\textit{Mitigation.}
Many of the analyzed projects are widely used in production and serve as dependencies for industrial systems.
This increases confidence that the results generalize beyond purely academic or hobbyist software.
Furthermore, since our method is language-agnostic, future work can investigate applications in other ecosystems.

\paragraph{Selection Bias}
The selection of projects may introduce bias if certain types of software or development practices are overrepresented.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We aimed to include a diverse set of projects by selecting repositories of varying sizes, domains and activity levels.
We also documented our selection criteria transparently to allow for replication and assessment of potential biases.

% --------------------- Section 4 ---------------------

\section{Conclusion Validity}\label{sec:conclusion-validity}

\paragraph{Reliability of Quantitative Results}
Our quantitative results depend on the accuracy of our data collection and analysis methods.
Errors in data extraction, measurement or statistical analysis could lead to incorrect conclusions.

\textit{Mitigation.}
We employed automated tools for data collection and analysis to minimize human error.
Additionally, we performed multiple runs of our experiments to ensure consistency in the results.
Where applicable, we used established statistical methods to analyze the data and increase confidence in our measurements.

\paragraph{Robustness of Qualitative Interpretations}
Our qualitative interpretations of the results may be influenced by subjective biases or misinterpretations of the data.

\textit{Mitigation.}
To mitigate this risk, we involved multiple researchers in the interpretation process.
We also cross-referenced our interpretations with existing literature to ensure alignment with established knowledge in the field.
